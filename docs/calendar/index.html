Google Calendar

Google Calendar opens with the calendar ready to add. Or, on a computer, open Settings, Add calendar, From URL and paste the link. It then shows on your phone too.

iPhone and iPad

Tap Apple Calendar on the phone and confirm. Or open Settings, Apps, Calendar, Calendar Accounts, Add Account, Other, Add Subscribed Calendar, and paste the link.

Mac and Outlook

On a Mac, Apple Calendar opens Calendar, or use File, New Calendar Subscription. In Outlook, use Add calendar, Subscribe from web, and paste the link.

Newroz and the two Eids follow other calendars, so they are listed for each year from 2020 to 2060. Eid dates use the arithmetic Hijri calendar and can differ by a day or two from the sighting of the moon.

How the Kurdish calendar works

The Kurdish calendar follows the sun. The year begins at Newroz, the March equinox, usually on 21 March. Months 1 to 6 have 31 days, months 7 to 11 have 30, and the last month has 29, or 30 in a leap year: the same lengths as the Persian calendar.

Only the year number differs. In the Kurdish solar numbering, the Kurdish year is the Persian year plus 1321. Counting from the fall of Nineveh in 612 BCE, it is the Persian year plus 1233.

Every date is turned into a Julian Day Number, a plain count of days, so any calendar converts to any other. The engine is tsroj, the same one the extension and the roj command use. The Islamic dates are arithmetic (tabular); where months begin with the sighting of the moon they can differ by a day or two.

Roj on your computer

On GNOME, Roj puts this calendar in the top bar, one click away, with your own events and any .ics calendar. The roj command gives the same answers in a terminal.

Install Roj Read the guide 
roj                  # today in all four calendars
roj cal --with g     # this Kurdish month
roj events --month   # this month's Kurdish days
roj ics --locale en > kurdish-events.ics
